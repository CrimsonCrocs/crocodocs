Open the final PDF and check its claims against your records, the current manual, and the event's submission instructions. Review the file judges will receive, not just the editable document.

## Evidence

Give the PDF to someone who did not write it. Ask them to find a mechanical decision, a software result, a member's learning, an outreach outcome, and the continuity plan. Let them read it before explaining anything aloud.

For each claim, check:

- The date, robot version, and season are clear where needed.
- A measurement identifies its units, conditions, and number of trials.
- A target or planned activity is not presented as completed work.
- Percentages and before-and-after comparisons use the correct baseline.
- The team's contribution is distinct from a mentor's, partner's, or library author's work.
- An award label points to evidence rather than standing in for it.

If you cannot support a number, remove it or rewrite the claim to match the records. Better wording cannot fix a missing result.

## BIOBUZZ limits

The following checks use **2026–27 BIOBUZZ Section 6, TU04**, updated October 8, 2026 and reviewed October 14, 2026. Recheck the current manual and later updates before an event.

| Check | Requirement or guidance |
| --- | --- |
| Cover | One cover page that identifies the team number. Cover content is not evaluated for award criteria. |
| Content | No more than fifteen content pages. Later pages are not reviewed. |
| Page size | US Letter, 8.5 × 11 inches, or A4, 210 × 297 mm. |
| Digital file | The complete submission must be **less than 15 MB**. |
| Season evidence | Progress, challenges, and accomplishments since **January 1, 2026**. Earlier seasons may provide growth context; keep the emphasis on current-season work. |
| Student privacy | Minimize personal information. Use first names and last initials; do not disclose students' full names. Student photos are permitted. |
| Readability | Avoid text below 10 pt and low-contrast text, including text placed on images. |
| Supporting material | Include essential evidence directly. Judges do not open portfolio links, websites, or videos, or retain extra interview handouts for deliberation. |
| Attribution | Credit outside work and AI or research assistance with the appropriate footnote or endnote. Respect intellectual property. |

The page and file limits are requirements. The font-size statement is readability guidance in A201's explanatory text. A PDF can meet the page count and still be too difficult to read.

## Export checks

Open the PDF on another device and inspect it at normal reading size. Check that fonts, symbols, equations, and images survived export. Print a representative dense page to test the body text and chart labels.

Count the cover and content pages separately. Confirm the team number on the cover, check the actual file size, and inspect the last content page for clipping. Compress oversized images without making evidence unreadable.

Remove comments, personal contact details, hidden drafts, and accidentally included pages. Keep an editable original and a dated copy of the submitted PDF.

## Submission

Follow the **Event Director's format and deadline** under A202. If the event gives no other instructions, the manual directs teams to submit one printed copy during the initial interview. Keep another copy available in the pit.

Check who is responsible for submitting, where the file or printed copy goes, and how the team will confirm delivery. An internal notebook or a link to a cloud folder does not automatically satisfy a portfolio submission instruction.

If local instructions use a different name for the document, ask the organizers what to submit and where. Check that the file still meets the official portfolio limits.

## Community and automated review

A peer, mentor, or portfolio-review channel can identify an unclear claim or an overlooked question. Ask for feedback on a specific issue, and verify advice about rules against current FIRST documents.

Check the source season, factual claims, and document-handling terms of any automated review tool. Its score does not predict the award result. Avoid sharing private student information that the review does not need.
