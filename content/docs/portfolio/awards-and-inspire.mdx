Build one portfolio for all the awards your team is pursuing. Show what you did, why you did it, and what came of it. Page references help judges find the details.

## Inspire

Inspire recognises a team that does well across robot design, team development, and the engineering process. To be considered, your team needs a portfolio and must be a strong contender for at least one award in each of these groups:

- **Machine, Creativity, and Innovation:** Design, Innovate, or Control.
- **Team Attributes:** Connect, Reach, or Sustain.
- **Think:** how you worked through engineering problems and what you learned.

Judges also look for a positive, inclusive team where everyone contributes and can explain the team's work. Give your robot, people, and outreach enough space for the reader to understand each.

## Evidence by award

Use this table to decide what to include. The examples are suggestions for showing the work behind each award.

| Award | What to explain | What you could show |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Think | How you solved an engineering problem | Your process, a lesson you applied, alternatives you compared, or a calculation that helped you choose a design |
| Connect | How members planned and developed their skills | Learning goals, training, advice from a STEM professional, and the work students completed afterward |
| Reach | How you helped grow FIRST | Outreach goals, the plan, your team's role, and people who joined FIRST as a result |
| Sustain | How the team plans to keep going and tracks its progress | A budget, recruitment and handover plans, progress reviews, and problems you prepared for |
| Innovate | How you developed a creative design that works reliably | Early ideas, prototypes, tests, and changes that helped it perform its job |
| Control | How feedback helps your robot perform better | The problem, sensors and other control components, what the code does with their readings, and test results |
| Design | How the whole robot works together | A labelled robot overview, design choices, efficient use of space, and access for repairs |

Think asks for at least one of the four engineering topics in its row. You do not need four separate stories. One well-explained design problem may cover several.

## Required and encouraged criteria

Read the required criteria for each award first, then the encouraged criteria. Use them to spot gaps in your portfolio. For example, a Connect page should explain the learning plan; a list of mentors alone leaves the reader wondering what members learned.

For Control, show how a sensor reading changes the robot's actions. For Design, explain how the mechanisms fit into the whole robot, even if one gets most of the page.

## Award map

A working table helps the team see what still needs writing. Keep it in your planning notes:

| Work | Awards it helps explain | Portfolio page | What to add |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lift redesign | Think, Design | Mechanism page | Test conditions and a comparison with the earlier lift |
| Feedback-controlled lift | Control | Software page | What happens if the home sensor fails |
| Mentor-led CAD training | Connect, Sustain | Team page | A part a student designed independently |
| Support for a new FLL team | Reach | Outreach page | How the team helped and whether the new team is participating |

Replace these examples with your own work and assign someone to fill each gap. Award labels can help with navigation, but the page still needs to tell the story.

## Team participation

Have members check the pages about their work. Everyone should be able to explain a contribution and something they learned, including members who handle outreach, finances, documentation, or manufacturing.

Ask someone outside each subteam to read its pages. If they cannot explain the decision afterward, add the missing context or explain an unfamiliar term.
