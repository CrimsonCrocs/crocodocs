List the awards your team is pursuing and the work that supports each criterion. Build one portfolio that covers them all, and use page references to help judges find the details.

## Inspire

In BIOBUZZ TU04, Inspire combines strength across **Machine, Creativity, and Innovation**, **Team Attributes**, and **Think**. Table 6-2 requires a portfolio, strong contention for at least one award in each of those groups, a positive and inclusive team in which every member contributes, and the ability to explain the team's experience to judges.

The technical group contains Design, Innovate, and Control. The Team Attributes group contains Connect, Reach, and Sustain. Think concerns the documented engineering journey.

Check all three groups when reviewing an Inspire draft. Explain how the robot developed, how members learned and kept the team running, and what the team contributed to FIRST. A strong section in one area can leave weak or missing explanations elsewhere.

## Evidence by award

The middle column below summarizes core criteria in Tables 6-3 through 6-9. The last column suggests ways to document them; those page formats are editorial advice.

| Award | Core criteria to address | Useful portfolio evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Think | A clear engineering record containing at least one of: process, applied lessons, comparison of alternatives, or math used in a design decision | A documented problem, alternatives, test results, the chosen change, and what the team learned |
| Connect | Learning goals and steps taken or planned to develop members' skills | A learning plan and a specific example of advice from a STEM professional being applied by students |
| Reach | Outreach objectives, strategy, contribution to FIRST growth, and successful recruitment of new FIRST participants | A project timeline, the team's role, evidence of recruitment, and follow-up with the people recruited |
| Sustain | A long-term sustainability plan and a way of tracking progress against it | A budget, recruitment or handover plan, progress reviews, and responses to organizational risks |
| Innovate | Engineering work behind a creative or original design that reliably supports game goals | Competing concepts, a mechanism explanation, test evidence, and how design risks were reduced |
| Control | Control components, the challenge and function of each, plus a solution that uses external feedback to improve robot performance | A sensor-to-action explanation, measured results, revisions, and recovery behavior |
| Design | Whole-robot design or its detailed development, including elegance, efficiency, or practical maintenance | A labelled robot overview, subsystem integration, packaging decisions, and service access |

For Think, the four engineering topics are alternatives under the required criterion; the manual does not ask you to manufacture four separate stories. A strong design investigation may naturally demonstrate several of them.

## Required and encouraged criteria

Check the **Required** rows first, then review the **Encouraged** rows. For Connect, explain the learning plan and show how professional relationships or collaboration helped members put it into practice.

For Control, explain how sensor feedback changes the robot's behavior. For Design, show how the whole robot fits together, even when you spend more space on one mechanism.

## Award map

Keep a working map outside the submitted portfolio:

| Evidence | Award connection | Where it appears | What is missing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lift redesign | Think, Design | Mechanism page | Test conditions and comparison with the earlier lift |
| Feedback-controlled lift | Control | Software page | Recovery from a failed home sensor |
| Mentor-led CAD training | Connect, Sustain | Team page | A member's independently completed design |
| New FLL team support | Reach | Outreach page | Confirmation of the team's role and current participation |

Replace these example entries with your team's work. Assign each missing item to a student and review the table as projects develop.

An award label can help a judge find a topic. The referenced page still needs to explain what happened and support the claim.

## Team participation

Have members review the sections about their work. Everyone should be able to explain a contribution and something they learned, including those working on outreach, finances, documentation, or manufacturing.

Ask someone outside each subteam to read its pages. If they cannot explain the decision or result afterward, check for missing context or undefined terms.
