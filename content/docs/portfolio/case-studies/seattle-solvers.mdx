Seattle Solvers, team 23511, received **Inspire second place in the Lovelace Division at the 2026 FIRST Championship**. Its DECODE portfolio describes member training, game strategy, and the performance measures used to guide robot revisions.

## Member development

Page 3 follows members from introductory training through offseason practice, independent work, and leadership. Examples of students taking responsibility show what they could do after training.

Page 2 connects team organization to a scheduling problem from the previous season. The revised plan is presented as a response to that experience. Recruitment and knowledge transfer are placed in the context of maintaining the team as its founding members graduate.

Choose a member's learning example and show the task they learned to complete independently. If an earlier training method failed, explain how you changed it.

## Performance measures

Pages 8–9 introduce measures the team uses to guide development, including travel time during a cycle, intake time, launch time, and scoring accuracy. These measures connect design changes to the team's game strategy.

Define the measure you use. For cycle time, state where the cycle starts and ends. For accuracy, define a successful attempt and keep all attempts in the denominator.

The portfolio credits a robotics professional for introducing this practice, showing where the team applied the advice.

## Goals and results

Page 15 identifies a **95% scoring-accuracy goal** and shows a reported progression from 57% to 94% across season stages. The latest point is 94%; the goal is 95%. Those are different statements.

Keep goals and results separate. Report the result at the document's date and explain the remaining work, even when the team is close to its target.

The graph needs a definition of the measurement and a trial count for readers to assess it fully. Label whether your own percentages come from matches, selected practice runs, or controlled tests.

## Design tradeoffs

Pages 9–11 compare robot approaches against the team's interpretation of the game and expected event conditions. The document discusses the cost of adding mechanisms as well as the capabilities they could enable.

Page 10 says the competing robot was still undecided at its April 6 writing date, after the release of Championship thresholds. This copy does not establish which architecture competed or how it affected the event result.

When your plan changes, record why. Keep a predicted advantage labelled as a prediction until the design is selected and tested.

## Software revisions

Page 15 follows several localization approaches, identifying concerns with camera observations, latency, mounting vibration, and odometry drift. It then describes combining information from odometry and vision.

The sequence explains why the team changed components and algorithms. For each change in your system, record the fault, the response, and the test of the revised version.

## Outreach and claims

Pages 5–7 describe recurring education, team support, and published resources. They contain a mixture of past activity and forward plans, so keep the dates and status visible when referring to them.

Usage totals and technical comparisons remain team-reported claims. Recheck historical statements about library maintenance before using them as current advice; see the [Programming section](/docs/libraries).

## Applying it

Choose one design goal and a measure that tells you whether you are meeting it. Record dated results across revisions and keep the target separate. Explain who learned the skills needed to make those changes.
