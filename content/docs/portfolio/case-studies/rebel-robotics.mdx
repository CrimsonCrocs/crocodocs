## Award overview

Content page 1 groups highlights under award headings and points to the detailed pages. It gives the reader an overview of the robot and the team before either gets a longer explanation.

For a similar overview, choose a few achievements and point to the pages that support them. Leave detailed investigations on those pages so the opening stays readable.

## Robot changes

Pages 9–10 follow changes from early competition versions to the Worlds robot. They connect specific observations to revisions: intake orientation limited pickup, transfers lost samples, slide returns needed improvement, and the scoring assembly carried excess weight.

Each version has a description of its problems and the changes that followed. Small charts show the team's reported throughput as the design developed. The pages explain why the mechanism changed.

Photograph failed parts before dismantling them. Keep the test result, the expected effect of the revision, and the following test together, including any failure that remains.

## Checking percentages

Page 10 compares a 3.4-second slide motion with a 1.1-second motion and labels the change as a 309% improvement. The two durations are useful; the improvement label needs clarification.

Using those reported durations, the motion takes about **68% less time**. Dividing 3.4 by 1.1 gives about **3.1**, a ratio between durations. Those calculations describe different quantities.

Check reliability claims the same way. Report the earlier and later failure counts under comparable conditions so readers can check the stated reduction.

## STEM connections

Page 6 names what professionals helped the team learn: design review, manufacturing drawings, electrical documentation, and mechanism ideas. Readers can see why the team sought each connection.

Pages 4–7 place those relationships within wider regional work. The portfolio separates different kinds of team support and describes continuing relationships with schools, industry, and local program leadership.

Choose a partnership and show what students did with the advice or support. Identify the partner's contribution separately. Include the resulting drawing, training, or project alongside any meeting photo.

## Technical detail

The mechanism pages use close-up views, labelled parts, and dimensions to show the features being discussed. A reader can find the part while reading why it changed.

Some broad performance and software comparisons on page 14 are not supported by a reproducible test on that page. Do not reuse its library judgments as current programming guidance. State the version and conditions when comparing software, and use current documentation for the actual APIs.

The pages are dense, with closely packed text and display lettering. Check body text at print size if you use a similar amount of material. Study the diagrams and comparisons without copying the page design.

## Applying it

Write a revision history for one subsystem: its objective, two versions, their test results, and the reason for the final choice. Add an example of mentor advice that changed the work, giving students and the mentor credit for their contributions.
