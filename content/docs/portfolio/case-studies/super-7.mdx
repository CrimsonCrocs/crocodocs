Super 7, team 7477, won the **Edison Division Inspire Award at the 2026 FIRST Championship**. Its DECODE portfolio covers team continuity and recurring outreach before comparing mechanism and control-system revisions.

## Page guide

This copy has one cover and fifteen numbered content pages. Some identifying details and images are redacted.

| 10–11 | Design priorities and a whole-robot overview |

## Team risks

Page 4 names specific continuity problems and places a response beside each: graduating members, missed deadlines, inexperienced recruits, and unavailable mentors. Training, shared responsibility, and progress reviews are described as responses to those problems.

The recruitment diagram shows how school programs lead to later participation. The training description explains how newer members practice on a training robot. Both give specific examples of how the team prepares for future seasons.

Start with a risk your team faces. Name who is learning the responsibility, how they practice, and which task they have completed independently.

## Outreach organization

Page 5 gives a broad timeline and overall figures. Page 6 selects larger projects for more explanation. Later pages distinguish community activities, technical exchanges, and mentor contributions.

The timeline shows the season at a glance; the project descriptions explain the work. Readers can find recurring local partnerships, published programming resources, and international projects separately.

Keep audience totals, resource users, event participants, and online viewers separate, with definitions and time periods. Explain what changed for participants alongside the attendance or usage figure.

Use the [outreach guide](/docs/portfolio/outreach) to distinguish activity, audience, recruitment, and impact when adapting this approach.

## Design comparisons

Page 12 places versions of the intake, shooter, and turret in columns. The images are accompanied by advantages, problems, and lessons. A reader can compare the same subsystem across versions without searching several pages.

Page 13 follows changes to the indexer's storage geometry, movement, wiring, and kickers. It describes how those changes affected failures and firing.

Use the same criteria when comparing your own versions. State what prompted each change and what the following test showed. Keep measurements separate from subjective ratings in a decision matrix.

## Control results

Page 15 connects launcher control to a specific problem: speed drops after a shot, affecting later shots. It places before-and-after plots beside the explanation and reports recovery time falling from roughly 1.2 seconds to 0.15 seconds.

Those are the team's reported results. The page connects a failure to the controller change and measured response. For a similar test, include the recovery tolerance, operating speed, hardware conditions, and number of trials.

Elsewhere, the portfolio pairs sensors and algorithms with their jobs. Explain those jobs when describing your system, rather than treating sensor count as a measure of control quality.

## Details to check

The reference includes dense pages and some incomplete or redacted mentor descriptions. Do not reconstruct those fields or treat the copy as a ready-to-use template. Review every label and remove draft text from your own export.

For claims about zero failures, accuracy, or time saved, state the tests and their conditions. Keep conclusions within what those trials establish and describe remaining problems.

## Applying it

Make a risk table for your team and compare two subsystem versions. Add the completed task, revised plan, or test that shows the response. Use an outreach timeline to leave room for the projects whose results need a longer explanation.
