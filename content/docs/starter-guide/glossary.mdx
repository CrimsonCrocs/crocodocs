These are working explanations for beginners. For a competition ruling, use the definition in the current Competition Manual.

## FIRST and the team

<dl className="starter-definitions">
  <dt>FIRST</dt><dd>For Inspiration and Recognition of Science and Technology, the organization behind several youth robotics programs.</dd>
  <dt>FTC</dt><dd>FIRST Tech Challenge, the program this guide is about.</dd>
  <dt>STEM</dt><dd>Science, technology, engineering, and mathematics.</dd>
  <dt>Rookie</dt><dd>A team or person in their first season of the program.</dd>
  <dt>Season</dt><dd>One yearly cycle of the program and its game.</dd>
  <dt>Kickoff</dt><dd>The release of the new season's game.</dd>
  <dt>Coach</dt><dd>An adult who helps organize and support the team.</dd>
  <dt>Mentor</dt><dd>Someone who shares experience and helps students learn.</dd>
  <dt>Program Delivery Partner</dt><dd>The organization that runs FIRST programs in a country or region.</dd>
  <dt>Gracious Professionalism</dt><dd>Doing good work while treating other people with respect.</dd>
  <dt>Coopertition</dt><dd>Helping others while also competing with them.</dd>
  <dt>Outreach</dt><dd>Work that shares the team's learning or opportunities beyond its members.</dd>
  <dt>Sponsor</dt><dd>A person or organization providing money, equipment, services, or other support.</dd>
  <dt>Budget</dt><dd>A plan for the money coming in and going out.</dd>
</dl>

## The robot

<dl className="starter-definitions">
  <dt>Chassis</dt><dd>The robot's main supporting frame.</dd>
  <dt>Drivetrain</dt><dd>The motors, wheels, and associated parts that move the robot.</dd>
  <dt>Mecanum</dt><dd>A wheel arrangement with angled rollers that can allow sideways movement.</dd>
  <dt>Strafe</dt><dd>Move sideways without first turning to face that direction.</dd>
  <dt>Mechanism</dt><dd>Connected parts arranged to perform a task.</dd>
  <dt>Intake</dt><dd>A mechanism that collects game objects.</dd>
  <dt>Actuator</dt><dd>A device that makes something move, such as a motor or servo.</dd>
  <dt>Motor</dt><dd>A device that turns electrical energy into motion.</dd>
  <dt>Torque</dt><dd>Turning force around an axis.</dd>
  <dt>RPM</dt><dd>Revolutions per minute, a measure of rotational speed.</dd>
  <dt>Gearbox</dt><dd>Gears arranged to change speed and torque between an input and an output.</dd>
  <dt>Servo</dt><dd>An actuator commonly commanded by position, or by speed in continuous-rotation mode.</dd>
  <dt>Shaft</dt><dd>A rod that turns and carries rotation to other parts.</dd>
  <dt>Bearing</dt><dd>A part that supports a shaft while allowing it to rotate.</dd>
  <dt>Fastener</dt><dd>A part that joins other parts, such as a screw or nut.</dd>
  <dt>Sensor</dt><dd>A device that measures something for the robot.</dd>
  <dt>Encoder</dt><dd>A sensor that measures rotation.</dd>
  <dt>IMU</dt><dd>Inertial measurement unit; a sensor package measuring motion and orientation.</dd>
  <dt>Robot Controller</dt><dd>The device running the robot code.</dd>
  <dt>Control Hub</dt><dd>A REV device combining a Robot Controller computer with connections for robot hardware.</dd>
  <dt>Expansion Hub</dt><dd>A REV device that provides additional hardware connections.</dd>
  <dt>Driver Station</dt><dd>The device used to select programs, start and stop them, and send gamepad input.</dd>
  <dt>Configuration</dt><dd>The record of devices, ports, and names used by the Robot Controller.</dd>
  <dt>Voltage, V</dt><dd>The electrical difference that drives current, measured in volts.</dd>
  <dt>Current, A</dt><dd>The flow of electric charge, measured in amperes.</dd>
  <dt>Polarity</dt><dd>Which electrical contact is positive and which is negative.</dd>
  <dt>Strain relief</dt><dd>Securing a cable so a pull is not taken by the connector.</dd>
  <dt>Stall</dt><dd>A powered motor being unable to turn.</dd>
</dl>

## Programming

<dl className="starter-definitions">
  <dt>Software</dt><dd>Programs and apps running on a computer or controller.</dd>
  <dt>Blocks</dt><dd>FTC's visual programming tool, using connected instruction shapes.</dd>
  <dt>Java</dt><dd>A text-based programming language supported for FTC robot code.</dd>
  <dt>OpMode</dt><dd>One selectable FTC robot program.</dd>
  <dt>Initialize / Init</dt><dd>Prepare a program and its hardware before the running phase.</dd>
  <dt>Input</dt><dd>Information a program reads, such as a button or sensor measurement.</dd>
  <dt>Output</dt><dd>Something the program controls, such as motor power.</dd>
  <dt>Variable</dt><dd>A named place to store a value.</dd>
  <dt>Condition</dt><dd>A question the code evaluates, such as whether a button is pressed.</dd>
  <dt>Loop</dt><dd>Instructions that repeat.</dd>
  <dt>Function</dt><dd>A named group of instructions that can be called.</dd>
  <dt>Timeout</dt><dd>A maximum allowed time before an action stops or reports failure.</dd>
  <dt>Simulator</dt><dd>Software that models a robot or environment so you can try an idea without the physical hardware.</dd>
  <dt>Backup</dt><dd>A separate saved copy you can recover if the working copy is lost.</dd>
</dl>

## CAD and engineering

<dl className="starter-definitions">
  <dt>CAD</dt><dd>Computer-aided design: making accurate drawings and models with software.</dd>
  <dt>Part Studio</dt><dd>An Onshape tab used to create parts.</dd>
  <dt>Sketch</dt><dd>A flat drawing used to define a part.</dd>
  <dt>Plane</dt><dd>A flat reference surface.</dd>
  <dt>Origin</dt><dd>The fixed point where the reference axes meet.</dd>
  <dt>Dimension</dt><dd>A measurement that controls or describes geometry.</dd>
  <dt>Constraint</dt><dd>A relationship that controls geometry, such as keeping two lines parallel.</dd>
  <dt>Extrude</dt><dd>Extend a flat region into a solid, or use it to remove material.</dd>
  <dt>Feature</dt><dd>A modelling operation, such as a sketch, extrusion, or cut.</dd>
  <dt>Assembly</dt><dd>Parts positioned together with their connections defined.</dd>
  <dt>Mate</dt><dd>A rule defining how two assembly parts can move relative to each other.</dd>
  <dt>Degree of freedom</dt><dd>An independent movement still available to a part.</dd>
  <dt>Version</dt><dd>A fixed snapshot of a design or set of files.</dd>
  <dt>BOM</dt><dd>Bill of materials: the parts and quantities in a design.</dd>
  <dt>COTS</dt><dd>Commercial off-the-shelf: a part bought ready-made.</dd>
  <dt>Prototype</dt><dd>An early model used to answer a design question.</dd>
  <dt>Iteration</dt><dd>Changing a design after learning from a test.</dd>
  <dt>Integration</dt><dd>Bringing separate parts of a robot together and testing them as one system.</dd>
  <dt>Tolerance</dt><dd>The allowed variation in a dimension.</dd>
  <dt>Clearance</dt><dd>An intended gap between parts.</dd>
  <dt>STL</dt><dd>A file format representing a surface with triangles, commonly used for 3D printing.</dd>
  <dt>STEP</dt><dd>A file format used to exchange precise geometry between CAD systems.</dd>
  <dt>DXF</dt><dd>A drawing exchange format often used for flat cutting outlines.</dd>
  <dt>Slicer</dt><dd>Software that turns a 3D model into printer instructions.</dd>
</dl>

## Competition and judging

<dl className="starter-definitions">
  <dt>Alliance</dt><dd>Teams playing together on one side in a match.</dd>
  <dt>Autonomous / auto</dt><dd>Robot operation without driver input.</dd>
  <dt>Teleop</dt><dd>The driver-controlled part of the match.</dd>
  <dt>Drive team</dt><dd>The people permitted to operate and support the robot at the field.</dd>
  <dt>Human player</dt><dd>A drive-team role performing the game's permitted human interactions.</dd>
  <dt>Cycle</dt><dd>One repeat of a task, such as collect, travel, score, and return.</dd>
  <dt>Penalty</dt><dd>A consequence for breaking a rule.</dd>
  <dt>Pit</dt><dd>A team's workspace at an event.</dd>
  <dt>Inspection</dt><dd>Checks that a robot and control system meet applicable rules.</dd>
  <dt>FTA</dt><dd>FIRST Technical Advisor, supporting technical operation of the competition field.</dd>
  <dt>Qualification matches</dt><dd>Scheduled matches with changing partners, used to establish team rankings.</dd>
  <dt>Playoffs</dt><dd>The later matches used to decide the event’s winning alliance.</dd>
  <dt>Advancement</dt><dd>Qualifying for a later event under the applicable system.</dd>
  <dt>Scouting</dt><dd>Observing other teams and recording what their robots can do.</dd>
  <dt>Queue</dt><dd>The place or process for assembling teams before a match.</dd>
  <dt>Referee</dt><dd>A volunteer who applies the game rules and determines scoring and penalties.</dd>
  <dt>Judge</dt><dd>A volunteer who evaluates team work for judged awards.</dd>
  <dt>Engineering notebook</dt><dd>The ongoing record of the team's work and decisions.</dd>
  <dt>Portfolio</dt><dd>A short selection of the team's work prepared for judging.</dd>
  <dt>Judging interview</dt><dd>A conversation where students explain their work and answer judges' questions.</dd>
  <dt>Team Update</dt><dd>An official announcement of changes or clarifications to the rules.</dd>
  <dt>Q&A</dt><dd>The official question-and-answer system for interpreting game rules.</dd>
</dl>
