Engineering turns a game task into a robot that can perform it repeatedly. The work includes deciding what to build, sizing its moving parts, making them fit, and testing the complete machine.

## First robot [#build-your-first-robot]

Start with [your first engineering build](/docs/engineering/first-build). It takes a kit drivetrain from an inventory of parts to a measured driving test. You do not need a full CAD model or custom manufacturing to begin.

Then use these articles in order:

1. [Game strategy](/docs/engineering/design/game-strategy): choose a task your team can deliver.
2. [Design process](/docs/engineering/design/design-process): turn that task into requirements and useful prototypes.
3. [Structure and fasteners](/docs/engineering/mechanical/structure-and-fasteners): build a frame that stays aligned.
4. [Motors and gearing](/docs/engineering/mechanical/motors-and-gearing): match motion to the load.
5. [Drivetrains](/docs/engineering/mechanisms/drivetrains) and [wiring and power](/docs/engineering/electrical/wiring-and-power): integrate the moving robot.
6. [Testing and reliability](/docs/engineering/team/testing-and-reliability): find failures before an event.

## Robot improvements [#improve-an-existing-robot]

Choose the problem you are actually seeing. A faster motor cannot fix an unreliable handoff, and a thicker plate cannot fix loose joints.

| Problem | Useful starting point |
| --- | --- |
| An arm struggles near horizontal | [Arms and linkages](/docs/engineering/mechanisms/arms-and-linkages) |
| A shaft moves sideways or a wheel binds | [Shafts and bearings](/docs/engineering/mechanical/shafts-and-bearings) |
| Gears bind, chain skips, or belts lose tension | [Gears](/docs/engineering/mechanical/gears), [belts and chain](/docs/engineering/mechanical/belts-and-chain) |
| A lift racks or its string wears | [Linear slides and rigging](/docs/engineering/mechanisms/linear-slides) |
| Pickup works only from one angle | [Intakes and grippers](/docs/engineering/mechanisms/intakes-and-grippers) |
| Pieces disappear between mechanisms | [Transfers](/docs/engineering/mechanisms/transfers) |
| Parts fit in CAD but need filing | [Fits and tolerances](/docs/engineering/manufacturing/fits-and-tolerances) |
| Printed parts crack around bolts | [3D printing](/docs/engineering/manufacturing/3d-printing) |
| A robot resets during combined motion | [Wiring and power](/docs/engineering/electrical/wiring-and-power) |
| Repairs require dismantling the robot | [Assembly and serviceability](/docs/engineering/team/assembly-and-serviceability) |

## Using the handbook [#use-the-handbook-with-your-robot]

Worked numbers are **illustrative design examples**, not tested performance claims or recommended hardware ratings. Replace masses, dimensions, efficiencies, and speeds with measurements from your own mechanism. Pages define essential terms locally; the [glossary](/docs/engineering/glossary) is a quick reference.

Engineering and programming meet at the hardware interface. Agree on travel limits, units, gear ratios, home positions, and sensor locations before tuning [PID control](/docs/control-systems/pid-control) or [motion profiles](/docs/control-systems/motion-profiling).

## Rules [#sources-and-rules]

The [resource guide](/docs/engineering/resources) explains which reference to use for each design decision. Supporting references appear in the Sources section at the bottom of each article.

Community engineering advice does not establish robot legality. Use the current official game and season materials, Team Updates, and FTC Q&A for competition requirements. This section avoids fixed season limits and approved-parts lists that would become stale.
