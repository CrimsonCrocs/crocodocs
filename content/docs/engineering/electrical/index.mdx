A good electrical system lets the robot move freely and makes faults easier to find. Start with a wiring diagram, a secure battery and switch, and cables you can trace without dismantling the robot.

## Wiring

[Wiring and power](/docs/engineering/electrical/wiring-and-power) covers the complete robot: port assignments, connector retention, current demand, and battery checks.

Use [connectors and cables](/docs/engineering/electrical/connectors-and-cables) when making or changing the harness. It covers connector families, crimps, moving cable runs, and labels. A connector that fits physically still needs the correct pinout and voltage.

## Faults

[Power faults and ESD](/docs/engineering/electrical/power-faults-and-esd) covers resets, shared servo power, voltage loss, static discharge, and noisy sensor readings. Work from the symptom and change one thing at a time.

For a sensor that reads correctly but gives the robot the wrong information, continue to [sensor integration](/docs/engineering/electrical/sensor-integration). Mounting, calibration, timestamps, and the meaning of a measurement matter as much as its electrical connection.

[Multi-turn position](/docs/engineering/electrical/multi-turn-position) explains how two geared absolute encoders can recover a turret's full turn count after a restart.

## Before driving

Move each mechanism through its full travel with power disconnected. Check that cables cannot enter rollers, stretch a connector, or get trapped between parts. Then test the robot's combined motions with its normal payload. A harness that survives one mechanism at a time may still fail when the lift, intake, and drivetrain run together.
