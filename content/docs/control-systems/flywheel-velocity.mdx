Control a flywheel's speed, then check that speed before feeding the next piece. Repeatedly changing PID gains will not fix an unplugged encoder, an unreachable speed, or a feeder that launches the next ball before the wheel recovers.

## Choose one controller

| Approach | Motor mode and command | Where the velocity feedback runs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hub velocity control | `RUN_USING_ENCODER` with `DcMotorEx.setVelocity()` | REV motor controller |
| Your own PID/feedforward | `RUN_WITHOUT_ENCODER` with `setPower()` | Your control loop |

`RUN_TO_POSITION` is a position target, not the normal choice for a continuously spinning flywheel. Do not run an external power-based velocity PID on top of the hub's velocity regulation by accident. Pick one owner and one interpretation of its output.

## Encoder units

The one-argument `setVelocity()` and `getVelocity()` use **ticks per second**, not RPM. Let $C_f$ be the encoder counts produced by one complete flywheel revolution, including any gearing between the encoder and flywheel:

$$
\text{ticks/s}=\frac{\text{flywheel RPM}\,C_f}{60},\qquad
\text{flywheel RPM}=\frac{60\,\text{ticks/s}}{C_f}
$$

Check whether a motor specification describes the encoder shaft or gearbox output. Do not apply the gearbox ratio twice. Verify the sign and counts by rotating the unloaded assembly a known amount before a powered test.

For example, suppose a measured assembly produces **28 counts per flywheel turn**. A target of 3,600 RPM becomes $3600\times28/60=1680$ ticks/s. A measured 1,540 ticks/s becomes $1540\times60/28=3300$ RPM, so the wheel is 300 RPM below target. The 28-count value is illustrative; using a gearbox-output specification for a different shaft changes the result.

These FTC SDK 12 fragments belong in an OpMode. Set `COUNTS_PER_FLYWHEEL_TURN` from your assembly, and `targetRpm` from a measured operating range:

```java
// During initialization. Import DcMotor and DcMotorEx.
DcMotorEx flywheel = hardwareMap.get(DcMotorEx.class, "flywheel");
flywheel.setMode(DcMotor.RunMode.RUN_USING_ENCODER);

// In the active control loop, after checking a finite, nonnegative target
// and a positive, finite COUNTS_PER_FLYWHEEL_TURN:
double requestedTicksPerSecond = targetRpm * COUNTS_PER_FLYWHEEL_TURN / 60.0;
flywheel.setVelocity(requestedTicksPerSecond);
double measuredTicksPerSecond = flywheel.getVelocity();
double measuredRpm = measuredTicksPerSecond * 60.0 / COUNTS_PER_FLYWHEEL_TURN;
```

Start with a low, mechanically appropriate test speed and a guarded flywheel. On cancellation and STOP, command zero velocity and zero power in the OpMode's cleanup. Verify that the encoder responds before increasing speed; a missing or reversed feedback signal can make a controller demand excessive output.

## Tune and measure

Begin with one flywheel at a fixed target. Record target speed, measured speed, battery voltage, commanded output when available, and the instant a ball enters. Measure unloaded regulation and loaded recovery separately.

For hub control, `setVelocityPIDFCoefficients(p, i, d, f)` configures its velocity gains. Those coefficients use the hub's controller conventions; they are not interchangeable with a custom controller's normalized-power gains.

For custom control, establish the sign with `error = target - measured`, then combine feedback with a fitted velocity/feedforward model. Check saturation before increasing integral gain. A motor already receiving its maximum available output cannot reach an arbitrarily high target. Use [system identification](/docs/control-systems/system-identification) for voltage-based coefficients and consistent battery compensation.

Two mechanically linked motors need compatible directions and targets. If one fluctuates, compare encoder traces, wiring, loading, and controller settings before declaring the motor defective. A common shaft can make two independently misconfigured controllers fight each other.

## Gate the feeder

Define a ready state from measured behaviour:

1. The requested speed is positive and inside the calibrated range.
2. The encoder data is valid and recent.
3. The measured speed remains within the chosen RPM tolerance for a settling interval.
4. The pose, turret, and hood satisfy their own conditions.

Reset that settling interval whenever speed leaves tolerance, the target changes, or a ball is fed. Permit one feed event, confirm that the piece cleared, then wait for recovery again. Give recovery a deadline and report a failure instead of waiting forever.

For an illustrative 3,600 RPM target, ±100 RPM band, and 0.15-second settling interval, a trace might look like this. Assume the other readiness conditions pass and the control loop keeps sampling between rows:

| Time after spin-up begins | Measured RPM | Speed gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0.20 s | 3,300 | Outside the band; not ready |
| 0.30 s | 3,540 | Enter the band; start settling |
| 0.40 s | 3,480 | Leave the band; reset settling |
| 0.50 s | 3,560 | Re-enter; start a new interval |
| 0.65 s | 3,590 | Ready if every intervening sample stayed in the band |
| 0.67 s, after one feed | 3,180 | Reset readiness and wait for measured recovery |

Checking only the first and last row cannot prove settling. A telemetry display may update less often than the control loop; evaluate the gate on each fresh control measurement.

A fixed delay may work over a narrow tested setup, but measured recovery adapts to the actual speed dip. Faster feeding can reduce accuracy if the wheel has not recovered. Compare hit rate and the complete multi-ball cycle time using the same ball condition, compression, range, and battery conditions.

## Diagnose the miss

| Observation | Investigate |
| --- | --- |
| Speed units are off by a constant factor | Encoder counts, RPM conversion, and gearing |
| Speed never reaches target and output saturates | Available motor speed/torque, battery, friction, and compression |
| First shot works; following shots fall short | Recovery, feeder timing, and ball contact |
| Speed recovers but shots still vary | Hood position, ball condition, alignment, and mechanical consistency |
| Tuning changes appear on screen but not on the robot | Whether control rereads the live fields and whether the dashboard integration matches the installed libraries |

For shot calibration, continue with [projectile mathematics](/docs/season-topics/projectile-mathematics). Add [shooting-on-the-move compensation](/docs/season-topics/shooting-on-the-move) after stationary shots and feeding are repeatable.
