Design starts with a question: where should this part go, how should it move, or how will we make it? **CAD**, short for computer-aided design, lets you answer those questions with a model before spending material and build time.

This section takes you from your first Onshape sketch to a complete robot layout. The examples use FTC parts and problems: sensor mounts, parallel-plate drivetrains, transfers, and crowded electronics bays.

## Start here

If you have never used CAD, work through these four guides in order. You will make a small mounting plate and assemble it with other parts.

1. [Onshape](/docs/design/onshape): accounts, documents, units, and finding your way around.
2. [Sketches](/docs/design/sketches): dimensions and constraints that keep a design predictable.
3. [Parts and variables](/docs/design/parts-and-variables): turn a sketch into a part you can change.
4. [Assemblies](/docs/design/assemblies): position parts and represent their movement.

For the wider job of starting a team, use the [Starter Guide](/docs/starter-guide). Its [first CAD lesson](/docs/starter-guide/cad-first-part) is a shorter introduction.

## Plan a robot

Once you can make and assemble simple parts, work out how the whole robot fits together.

| Guide | What you will work out |
| --- | --- |
| [Layout sketches](/docs/design/layout-sketches) | Wheel positions, mechanism locations, and the game-piece path |
| [Robot packaging](/docs/design/packaging) | Motion clearance, wiring, battery changes, and repair access |
| [Custom drivetrains](/docs/design/custom-drivetrains) | Axles, bearings, transmissions, and side plates |
| [Mechanism design](/docs/design/mechanism-design) | Required motion, useful prototypes, and interfaces between mechanisms |

Start with simple shapes. A rectangle reserving room for the battery is useful long before you model its latch. Add detail when it answers a fit, motion, strength, or manufacturing question.

## Get ready to build

A finished model needs more than an attractive view. Someone must be able to buy its hardware, make its custom parts, and put it together.

- [Design for manufacturing](/docs/design/design-for-manufacturing): choose shapes your team can make and inspect.
- [Drawings and exports](/docs/design/drawings-and-exports): prepare files, dimensions, and a parts list.
- [CAD teamwork](/docs/design/cad-teamwork): share references and keep the model matched to the robot.
- [CAD examples](/docs/design/cad-examples): study FTC teams' design decisions with credited images and videos.

The [Engineering handbook](/docs/engineering) covers the mechanics behind these designs, including motors, gears, materials, wiring, and testing. Use both sections together: CAD can show that parts fit, while a physical test tells you whether the mechanism works.
