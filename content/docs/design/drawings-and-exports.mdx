Use the **70 × 40 × 3 mm Plate** from [Parts and variables](/docs/design/parts-and-variables). You will make a dimensioned drawing and export its shape. If you completed [Assemblies](/docs/design/assemblies), you can also produce a four-part bill of materials.

## Choose the file

| Format | What it carries | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| STEP | Solid geometry | Sharing with another CAD tool or a machinist |
| DXF | A flat profile | Cutting a sheet part |
| STL or 3MF | A surface mesh, made of small flat faces | A supported 3D-printing workflow |
| PDF drawing | Views, dimensions, and notes | Communicating requirements and checking a part |
| CSV | Table values | Purchasing or reviewing a parts list |

Keep the editable Onshape document. Exported geometry does not preserve its original feature history. Ask the recipient which format their process accepts.

## Make a plate drawing [#make-a-useful-drawing]

1. Open the Plate Part Studio. In the **Parts list**, right-click Plate and choose **Create drawing of Plate**.
2. Choose a millimeter drawing template with a suitable sheet size, such as A4. Use **Suggested views** to begin with views of the part.
3. Keep a face-on view that shows both holes and an edge view that shows thickness. Delete redundant views if they crowd the sheet. Move the views apart before adding dimensions.
4. Use **Dimension** to show the overall **70 mm width**, **40 mm height**, and **3 mm thickness**.
5. Dimension a hole to **4.5 mm diameter** and add a note that it applies to **both through holes**.
6. Locate the first hole center **15 mm from the left edge** and **20 mm from the bottom edge**. Show **40 mm between centers**. These locate the second hole without another chain of duplicate dimensions.
7. Add the part name and a revision, such as `Plate — r1`. Check the drawing's displayed units and scale.

For an actual manufacturing order, add the chosen material, stock thickness, quantity, and functional tolerances. A bearing seat needs a different fit decision from a loose screw hole. Use [Fits and tolerances](/docs/engineering/manufacturing/fits-and-tolerances) to establish those requirements with the fabricator.

Right-click the drawing tab, choose **Export**, and select **PDF**. Open the downloaded PDF and check that dimensions, notes, and the title block are legible. A drawing scale changes how a view appears on paper; its dimension values still describe the real part.

## Export the plate [#export-one-revision]

For a 3D-printing trial:

1. Return to Plate and right-click the **part in the Parts list**, not the sketch. Choose **Export**.
2. Select **STL**, **Millimeter**, and a suitable mesh resolution. Use a filename such as `practice-plate-70x40x3-r1.stl`.
3. Open it in the intended printer's slicer. Confirm **70 × 40 × 3 mm** and two open holes before choosing print settings.

For a flat cutting profile, right-click the plate's large **planar face** and choose **Export as DXF/DWG**. Select DXF and millimeters. Exporting the face gives its outside boundary and hole boundaries without the drawing's annotations.

You can [download a reference DXF](/guides/design/practice-plate-70x40.dxf) to compare with your result. It is an original 2D profile of the same practice geometry: one 70 × 40 mm closed rectangle and two circles of radius 2.25 mm. It is not a substitute for your editable model.

Open the DXF in the receiving tool and measure 70 mm across the outline and 40 mm between circle centers. If the size is wrong, correct the import/export units before cutting. Confirm whether the fabricator applies kerf or tool compensation; avoid applying it twice.

## Build a parts list

A **bill of materials**, or **BOM**, lists the parts and quantities in an assembly. Open `Practice assembly`, then select the **BOM Table** icon on the right side of the Assembly workspace.

Choose **Flattened** to combine repeated instances. Add the Name column if needed. For this exercise the list should contain:

| Part | Quantity | Geometry |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plate | 2 | 70 × 40 × 3 mm; two 4.5 mm holes |
| Spacer | 2 | 10 mm OD, 4.5 mm ID, 12 mm length |

**OD** means outside diameter; **ID** means inside diameter. The two plate instances count as two of the same part, not two separately designed parts. If the count is wrong, check the Assembly instance list.

Fill in part numbers and descriptions through each part's **Properties**. For practice, names such as `PLATE-001` and `SPACER-001` are enough; purchased components need their actual supplier part numbers.

Use the BOM table's **three-dot menu → Export to CSV**. Open the CSV in a spreadsheet and check the two quantities. The [reference parts list](/guides/design/practice-assembly-bom.csv) shows the expected geometry count. Add real fasteners and assembly supplies before treating a BOM as a complete build order.

## Revision files [#specify-the-fits-that-matter]

Create a named version after accepting the final geometry. Keep the exported profile, 3D model, drawing, and BOM tied to that version. If a hole moves later, update the affected drawing and exports before someone makes the old plate.
