# BIOBUZZ presented by RTX

BIOBUZZ is the 2026–27 FIRST Tech Challenge game in the FIRST CANOPY season. Two alliances, each made up of two teams, collect **POLLEN** and **NECTAR**, launch them into their alliance's **HIVE**, place game pieces into **FLOWERS**, and score in their **GARDEN**.

This is a guide to the game at a glance. The [official Competition Manual](https://ftc-resources.firstinspires.org/ftc/archive/2027/game) is the source of truth. At the time this page was reviewed, the current manual was **TU04**, updated October 8, 2026. Recheck the [current game resources](https://ftc-resources.firstinspires.org/ftc/game) for later Team Updates before every event.

## Match flow

A match consists of a **30-second AUTO period**, an **8-second transition**, and a **2-minute TELEOP period**. During AUTO, robots run pre-programmed instructions. During TELEOP, drivers control the robots. As the match progresses, teams launch game pieces to tip their HIVE, earn access to additional NECTAR, fill FLOWERS, and park in the LOADING ZONE.

![BIOBUZZ match timeline showing 30 seconds of autonomous play, an 8-second transition, and 2 minutes of driver-controlled play.](/guides/biobuzz-match-timeline.svg)

The transition separates the two scoring periods; drivers begin controlling their robots in TELEOP. See [Section 10.4 of the manual](https://ftc-resources.firstinspires.org/ftc/archive/2027/game) for the official timing.

## Game pieces and field elements

- **POLLEN** is the yellow game piece.
- **NECTAR** is alliance-colored red or blue.
- **HIVE** is the alliance structure with two pivoting **CELLS**. Launching into the upward-facing CELL can tip the HIVE.
- **FLOWERS** are field structures filled with POLLEN and NECTAR. Alliance ownership and scoring depend on placement defined in the manual.
- **GARDEN** is an alliance scoring area for POLLEN and NECTAR.
- **LOADING ZONE** is also where robots can earn PARK points.

The manual describes field setup, object dimensions, legal interactions, and scoring volumes in Sections 9–11. Use those diagrams for mechanism design rather than relying on this short glossary.

## Scoring at a glance

The manual awards points for HIVE tips, game pieces in CELLS, game pieces in FLOWERS and GARDENS, LEAVE, and PARK. For example, its current point table assigns **20 points per HIVE TIP**, **2 points per POLLEN or NECTAR remaining in an upward-facing CELL at the end**, and **5 points for the bottom-NECTAR FLOWER bonus**. The exact conditions matter: read the scoring criteria and game rules before treating a piece as scored.

BIOBUZZ has three game-specific Ranking Points—**SWARM RP**, **POLLINATOR 1 RP**, and **POLLINATOR 2 RP**—as well as the match WIN/TIE Ranking Points. The latest manual table lists Championship thresholds as to be announced and thresholds for other events; Team Updates may change these numbers. Check the live manual before setting season goals.

## Strategy and programming

Start by making a robot that can complete one repeatable task safely. Then test a short AUTO routine, measure cycle time, and coordinate with your alliance partner. HIVE tipping and FLOWER scoring have timing and interaction rules, so design the mechanism around the official drawings and criteria rather than an animation alone.

For software, separate hardware control from match logic, log sensor and mechanism state during testing, and make autonomous behavior stop safely when a sensor reading or mechanism state is unexpected. See [Autonomous](./autonomous), [Vision](./sensors-and-vision/vision-overview), and [Control Systems](./control-systems) for general programming concepts. ## AprilTag clusters in BIOBUZZ

BIOBUZZ uses AprilTag **clusters** on HIVE CELLS. Each CELL has a four-tag cluster; FTC documents four defined clusters for the game and no standalone game tags. The SDK reports a visible cluster as one `AprilTagClusterDetection`, even if it sees one or several of that cluster's printed tags. The cluster's pose can point toward the CELL opening rather than toward the center of a printed tag. This matters when using camera pose to line up a scoring action.

SDK 12.0 and later return both single-tag and cluster detection types. Check the type before reading type-specific fields; cluster results have a name and `percentClusterFound`, not a single tag ID. See the [step-by-step AprilTag guide](./sensors-and-vision/visionportal/apriltag-detection) and [official FTC cluster guide](https://ftc-docs.firstinspires.org/en/latest/tech_tips/tech-tips/tech-tip-apriltag-clusters/tech-tip-apriltag-clusters.html).
